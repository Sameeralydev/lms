# International Policy Profiles

## Functionality

Institutions may opt in to jurisdiction/framework profiles. A profile identifies country/region, institution type, framework/authority reference, and a version. Versions can carry calendar, grading, qualification, credit applicability, effective-date, and review metadata. Dated evidence, requirement mappings, explicit gap records, and gap resolutions are persisted with tenant scope. A version cannot be marked reviewed/selected as verified without dated evidence and at least one mapped requirement; the feature test covers this gate.

Profiles are empty institution-controlled records, not built-in country law. Pakistan, UK, US, Gulf, Australian, and other rules are not represented as authoritative defaults. A tenant reviewer must add an authoritative source, date, and mapping; qualified jurisdictional review remains necessary.

## Source and terminology references

The data shape is intended to accommodate qualification terminology informed by UNESCO UIS [ISCED](https://www.uis.unesco.org/en/methods-and-tools/isced) and credit terminology informed by the European Commission [ECTS](https://education.ec.europa.eu/education-levels/higher-education/inclusive-and-connected-higher-education/european-credit-transfer-and-accumulation-system). These references do not make a profile applicable to an institution or establish legal compliance.

## Limits

No authority-specific rules, automatic legal updates, public profile catalog, profile import, or automated jurisdictional conformance check is delivered. The profile UI includes a few optional text fields and workflow prompts; it is an administrative record, not a regulatory engine.
